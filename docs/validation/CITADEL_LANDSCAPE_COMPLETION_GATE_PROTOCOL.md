## Participants and Session Setup

Start with a small practical set, for example four to six uninvolved visitors. This is a scheduling suggestion, not a pass threshold or a representative survey. Note missing perspectives and mixed results instead of converting a small sample into population claims.

Where practical, include different levels of NIN9ONE familiarity, music familiarity, design or interactive-culture familiarity, and people with no project context. Include actual intended devices. Use desktop pointer and real mobile touch as the initial coverage plan based on the implemented responsive surface; record the device classes actually intended for the audience before drawing a readiness conclusion. A high-end desktop or emulated phone cannot stand in for missing real-device evidence.

For each session:

- Confirm the visitor was not involved in building the Citadel; record prior visits, previews, or briefing. A prior guided exposure is not fresh first-contact evidence. Ask detailed familiarity questions after the visit to avoid introducing lore or the intended product framing beforehand.
- Record participant alias, served revision, access URL or local launch, entry route, device/model, browser, viewport/orientation, input method, sound output, motion preference, and any relevant access needs the visitor chooses to share. Keep personal contact details out of the repository.
- Use the unchanged experience with a fresh session for existing discovery state. Enter through the normal home route unless the actual delivery starts elsewhere; record any deviation and keep first-contact observations comparable.
- Verify access before the participant begins. Local supervised access is sufficient; this protocol does not require a new deployment, publication, outreach message, or analytics system.
- Explain note-taking and obtain agreement for any recording. Written observation is enough; video is optional. The visitor may stop at any time.
- Preserve the normal opt-in sound and reduced-motion controls. Do not switch them on or off for the visitor's first contact.

Builder rehearsals and automated browser checks can establish that the session works technically. They are not participant evidence.

## First-Contact Sequence

### 1. Minimal framing

Use only:

> “Please explore this as you normally would. Take your time, and let me know when you feel finished. You can stop whenever you like.”

Do not introduce the Citadel as a destination, explain controls, point out responses or entrances, describe the music, or teach the six dimensions. Do not require continuous think-aloud; capture spontaneous comments without turning the visit into a task list.

### 2. Unassisted visit

Allow a short natural visit, usually around five to ten minutes, with no required dwell time. Record actual duration and who ended it. Note the order of attention, movement attempts, approaches, retreats, pauses, discoveries, sound choices, and difficulties. Keep observed actions and exact words separate from interpretation.

When asked what to do, reply neutrally: “What would you try?” If help is needed, provide it and record its timing and content. Subsequent behavior is assisted; it cannot retroactively establish unaided legibility. Do not redesign, change the build, or coach the next participant around a weakness during the observation batch.

### 3. Exit and memory first

Remove the experience from view before asking:

> “What stays with you from what you just visited?”

Capture the initial answer before any detailed questioning. A later neutral follow-up can ask, “Is there anything else you remember?” Note the interval since exit. Do not supply images, landmark names, or response examples. Memory here concerns what remains after leaving this session; it supplies no evidence of repeat visits, long-term retention, residue, or return cadence.

### 4. Neutral retrospective prompts

After the first memory response, ask only the prompts needed to clarify what was observed:

- “How would you describe what you just used to someone else?”
- “What drew your attention? Where, if anywhere, did you want to go?”
- “What did moving feel like? What changed as you moved?”
- “How did you experience distance and size?”
- “What, if anything, seemed to respond to you? What led you to think that?”
- “Where, if anywhere, did it seem possible to go further? What gave you that impression?”
- “What different parts of the surroundings do you remember?”
- “Was anything difficult to use or uncomfortable?”
- “What, if anything, made this worth spending time with?”

Record these answers as prompted retrospective evidence, separate from spontaneous behavior. Do not ask the visitor to score the gate, choose a verdict, or design the next feature. A desire to return may be recorded as stated interest, never as observed return behavior.

### 5. Supplemental controls and context

After first-contact and memory evidence are captured, clarify prior familiarity. If needed, inspect missed essential controls, sound opt-in/reversal, reduced motion, keyboard and assistive access, or a reported performance issue in a clearly labeled assisted phase. These checks inform readiness and diagnostics, not unaided discovery. Stop if uncomfortable.

## Visitor Spine — Observer Reference

Do not show this table to visitors before their session. For each row, preserve supporting evidence, contrary evidence, and what remains unknown. No numeric score or average is required.

| Signal | Evidence to look for | Limits and contrary evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Destination | Attention and approach naturally organize around the Citadel; the visitor identifies a significant place toward which they want to move. | Naming a large object after prompting does not establish destination. The visitor need not know its canonical symbolism. |
| Journey | The visitor experiences changing spatial relationships, foreground, distance, or route through a place while moving. | “Rotating a model” or “zooming an image” despite successful controls is relevant contrary evidence. Camera motion alone is insufficient. |
| Scale | Approach changes the visitor's sense of distance, monumentality, and physical presence. | An impressive first still or increased pixel size alone does not establish approach-dependent scale. |
| Responsiveness | The visitor relates attention or movement to coherent environmental answers across the established experience; note atmosphere, Black Water, sound, light, reflection and discovery where encountered. | One functioning reflection cannot establish the whole condition. Identify systems experienced, missed, muted, or technically checked. Sound left off is a valid choice; unexperienced systems remain unknown, not inferred successes. |
| Exterior Threshold Legibility | The visitor perceives a plausible place of entry and shows approach or anticipation toward it; record the physical cue they refer to. | Mere curiosity about a distant building is insufficient if no plausible entrance is perceived. Crossing or changing the interior experience is outside this gate. |
| Memory | After exit, an unprompted specific image, location, event, feeling tied to a place, question, or inaccessible possibility remains. | Generic praise and recognition from supplied screenshots are weak evidence. No claim about long-term retention or repeat visitation follows. |

## Readiness Rails — Required Alongside the Spine

| Condition | Evidence to collect | Reason to withhold closure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surrounding-environment distinction | Visitors distinguish spatial or environmental relationships beyond the central silhouette and describe surroundings as somewhere. | Attractive background detail without meaningful distinction leaves the Citadel isolated in a tableau. Exhaustive exploration is not required. |
| Place versus demo | Unprimed descriptions and movement suggest an explorable place, supported by what visitors actually did. | Technical novelty, camera manipulation, passive cinematic viewing, or promotional framing dominates. A single preferred word is not a pass test. |
| Standalone value | Visitors with no music or lore context find curiosity, atmosphere, or meaning without explanation. | Only informed fans find value, or facilitation supplies the meaning. Missing context-free visitors is a coverage gap. |
| Intended-device performance and essential controls | Actual intended devices sustain atmosphere and usable navigation, discovery, reflection, opt-in audio/reversal, and relevant access paths. Record load problems, stalls, input delay, touch difficulty, discomfort, control failures and their impact. | Desktop-only success, significant control friction, inaccessible essential actions, or missing material device classes prevents a supported readiness claim. Emulation and technical checks are supplementary. |

Responsiveness and readiness should be considered together: record both the visitor's experience of environmental answers and technical stability of the established systems. Do not average away a broken essential control or infer system-wide responsiveness from the latest Black Water change.

Save original observations before writing cross-session interpretation. Preserve contrary cases and prompted/unprompted distinctions. Do not rewrite quotes to fit a conclusion. If using a recording, note relevant timestamps and its access location; avoid committing identifying recordings to the repository.

Freeze the experience throughout the batch. If a material outage or unavoidable revision change occurs, record it, separate the affected sessions, and retain their limits. Do not mix differently served behavior under one baseline.

## Move 3 — Evidence Package for Founder Exterior Disposition

After actual sessions, produce a concise synthesis with:

1. Baseline, participant/device coverage, assistance, missing evidence and limitations.
2. Findings for all six signals and all four readiness conditions, each linked to raw evidence and counterexamples. Counts such as “two of five sessions” may describe recurrence but do not establish statistical thresholds.
3. One of the three recommendations below, the reason, and the smallest next decision required. Record the Founder's resulting disposition separately from Codex's recommendation.

| Recommendation | Basis | What follows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PASS | Evidence supports the complete visitor spine and readiness rails; material gaps or contradictions have been resolved sufficiently for Founder judgment. | Founder may close the exterior proof and separately consider Threshold + First Authored Encounter. No interior permission is implied. |
| PASS WITH ONE BOUNDED CORRECTION | One sufficiently repeated, specific deficiency prevents closure; the remaining gate is supported. | Describe one targeted correction for separate authorization and the affected condition to reconsider afterward. No general polish window; the gate does not close on the proposal alone. |
| NOT YET | Fundamental conditions fail, or missing/contradictory evidence prevents a defensible conclusion. Distinguish demonstrated failures from insufficient evidence. | Interior remains closed. Findings may support a separately authorized exterior window or completion of missing observation. |

Absent sessions are a collection dependency, not a completed Move 3 verdict. No recommendation has been made in this protocol. Visitor requests remain evidence for Founder product judgment; quests, HUDs, inventories, progression and similar conventions do not become requirements through feedback.
