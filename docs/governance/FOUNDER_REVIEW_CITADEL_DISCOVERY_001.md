## 2. What I Learned

- The strongest discovery behavior is world-space attention followed by environmental response.
- Distinct illumination and rupture responses make the scene feel reactive without adding new meaning.
- Hiding names until attention improved discovery, but the persistent numbered landmark interface still framed the experience as a tracked set.
- The interface remained more directive and progression-like than the intended experience of encountering a place.
- Lore presentation explained the project thesis too directly and exposed internal classification language that belongs in governance rather than the player experience.

## 3. Current Experience

Lore panels expose internal classification language such as `Canon fragment` and `Atmospheric reading`. These presentation choices are now rejected by the Founder and are not the approved direction for further development.

## Founder Disposition — Citadel Discovery Surface

### Approved direction

- The Citadel is encountered primarily as a place, not as a tracked set of landmark signals.
- Discovery emerges through observation, proximity, focus, intentional interaction, environmental response, subtle illumination, rupture, and material response.
- UI remains contextual, temporary, and subordinate to the world.
- Existing discovery-state architecture may remain where useful, but it must not become collectible-style progression.

### Rejected direction

- A persistent numbered landmark checklist or signal HUD as the primary discovery surface.
- Presenting all discoverable landmarks to the player in advance.
- Player-facing internal labels such as `Canon fragment`, `Atmospheric reading`, or canon classifications.
- Lore that explicitly teaches the Traveler / Seeker thesis rather than suggesting meaning.

### Authority boundary

This disposition changes presentation direction only. It does not add, revise, resolve, or authorize new canon.
