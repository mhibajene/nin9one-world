## 2. What I Learned

- Removing the persistent list immediately made the Citadel read more as a place and less as a set of tasks.
- The useful part of the discovery architecture was the shared attention and response state, not its visible progression surface.
- Shorter lore materially improved the balance between observation and interpretation. The previous copy explained established symbolism before the player had space to infer it.
- Clean-session comparison revealed that the contextual cue still carried most of the discovery feedback because the existing world-space lights were effectively imperceptible at values between roughly `3` and `6`.
- Calibrated local light plus existing gold and rupture material traces gives attention a readable environmental consequence without turning landmarks into markers.
- The visually hidden native-button route preserves keyboard and assistive operation, and focus produces the same world response and contextual name. Its discoverability for a sighted keyboard-only player remains worth observing because the control itself is intentionally not persistent UI.

## 3. Current Experience

The Citadel now opens as an uninterrupted atmospheric place. No visible list announces the number, names, or completion state of its discoverable forms.

When attention reaches an existing landmark, the world responds locally through illumination or rupture and a restrained temporary name appears. Intentional interaction opens a short reflection. The reflection does not expose governance classifications or progression state and no longer recites the Traveler / Seeker thesis.

Discovery and revisit state still persist for the browser session, but that state is expressed through the world's material response rather than a collectible-style interface. The three existing landmarks, their classifications, and all canon remain unchanged.
